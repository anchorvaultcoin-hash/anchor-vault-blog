Your wallet’s already been compromised. You just don’t know it yet.

2026-08-01

Think you’re safe?

Hackers figured out it’s more profitable to hack people than to hack code.

You’ve got a wallet. You keep your seed phrase in a secure place. You double-check addresses before sending. Good for you.

But here’s the 2026 stat: the average loss from a single key theft is $13 million. From a smart contract exploit — under $1 million.

Hackers figured it out: breaking people pays better than breaking code.

What happened to those who were “careful enough” Humanity Protocol. A 3-of-6 multisig on Ethereum, 3-of-5 on BNB. All the keys sat on one employee’s laptop. When that got compromised — the hacker got everything. $36 million gone overnight.

Coldcard. Nearly 1,200 addresses, $70 million. The problem wasn’t phishing — it was key generation. People did everything “right,” and their money still vanished.

Drift Protocol. $285 million. A six-month operation to earn the team’s trust. The code was flawless. The people weren’t.

The takeaway is simple: multisig is useless if all signers use the same interface, store keys in the same place, and don’t verify transactions independently.

What’s your problem right now? You’re holding funds in a wallet tied to a single key.

If that key gets stolen — the hacker gets everything. Doesn’t matter how you store it: on paper, in a Ledger, in a safe. If the key is compromised, you’re wiped out.

And the worst part: you won’t even know until it’s too late.

Hackers don’t break into your wallet. They wait for you to slip up. And then they take it all.

What AnchorVaultCoin offers Not multisig. Not “another wallet.” A vault.

AnchorVaultCoin is a non-custodial storage built on two independent keys:

Primary key — for everyday transactions.

Emergency key — for withdrawals.

If an attacker steals your primary key (seed phrase, wallet, laptop), they can’t move funds without a signature from the second key.

And you keep that second key somewhere separate — on paper, in a bank deposit box, with someone you trust.

This isn’t multisig. It’s a separation of duties that makes a single-device attack pointless.

How it works in 30 seconds Set up a backup address — your cold wallet, where funds go in an emergency.

Create two keys — primary and emergency. Store them in different places.

Sleep easy — even if your wallet gets compromised, the vault stays locked.

And yes, emergency withdrawal is instant — no signature needed, 80% of funds go to your backup address. When every second counts.
