*Hackers figured out it's more profitable to hack people than to hack code.*

You've got a wallet. You keep your seed phrase in a secure place. You double-check addresses before sending. Good for you.

But here's the 2026 stat: **the average loss from a single key theft is $13 million**. From a smart contract exploit — under $1 million.

Hackers figured it out: breaking people pays better than breaking code.

What happened to those who were "careful enough"
Humanity Protocol. A 3-of-6 multisig on Ethereum, 3-of-5 on BNB. All the keys sat on one employee's laptop. When that got compromised — the hacker got everything. $36 million gone overnight.

Coldcard. Nearly 1,200 addresses, $70 million. The problem wasn't phishing — it was key generation. People did everything "right," and their money still vanished.

Drift Protocol. $285 million. A six-month operation to earn the team's trust. The code was flawless. The people weren't.

The takeaway is simple: multisig is useless if all signers use the same interface, store keys in the same place, and don't verify transactions independently.

What's your problem right now?
You're holding funds in a wallet tied to a single key.

If that key gets stolen — the hacker gets everything. Doesn't matter how you store it: on paper, in a Ledger, in a safe. If the key is compromised, you're wiped out.

And the worst part: you won't even know until it's too late.

Hackers don't break into your wallet. They wait for you to slip up. And then they take it all.
